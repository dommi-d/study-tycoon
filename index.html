    Video poker · jacks or better

    New to poker? How to play (tap to hide)

    This is video poker. You play alone against the paytable, not against other people. The goal is to end with a good five-card poker hand.

    Each round

    1. Bet. Type an amount (or use 1/2, 2x, Max) and press Deal. You get five cards.
    2. Hold. Tap the cards you want to keep. Held cards lift up and say HOLD.
    3. Draw. Press Draw. Every card you did not hold is swapped for a new one, once.
    4. Get paid. If your final five cards make a hand in the table below, you win your bet times the number on the right. Otherwise you lose the bet.

    Winning hands, best to worst

    HandWhat it looks likePays
    Royal flush10, J, Q, K, A, all the same suit250x
    Straight flushFive in a row, all the same suit (like 5-6-7-8-9)50x
    Four of a kindFour cards of the same rank (like four 8s)25x
    Full houseThree of a kind plus a pair (like 9-9-9-4-4)9x
    FlushAny five cards of the same suit6x
    StraightFive in a row, any suits. The ace works high (10-J-Q-K-A) or low (A-2-3-4-5)4x
    Three of a kindThree cards of the same rank3x
    Two pairTwo different pairs (like K-K-7-7)2x
    Jacks or betterOne pair of Jacks, Queens, Kings or Aces. Lower pairs do not pay.1x (bet back)

    Easy tips

    • Always keep a hand that already pays, like two pair, three of a kind, a straight or a flush.
    • Keep any pair. A pair of Jacks or better already pays, and a lower pair is a good start toward three of a kind.
    • With four cards to a flush (four of one suit) or four in a row, hold those four and draw one. It does not always land, but it pays well when it does.
    • With no pair, keep your Jacks, Queens, Kings and Aces. Pairing one of them pays.
    • If nothing helps, hold nothing and draw five fresh cards.
    • Start with small bets while you learn. Most hands lose, and wins come in streaks.

    Markets · each contract pays 100 if right

    simulated events, play money
    How market payouts work (tap to hide)

    Each market asks a yes/no question. The price in cents is the crowd's chance that the answer is YES. A YES contract at 41¢ costs 41 chips. If the market settles YES, every YES contract pays 100 chips back, so your profit is 59. If it settles NO, it pays nothing and you lose the 41. NO contracts work the other way around and cost 100 minus the YES price.

    Cheap contracts pay more when they hit but hit less often. A 1¢ contract returns 100 if right (99 profit). A 91¢ contract returns the same 100 but only 9 profit.

    Each buy and each early sell pays a small fee, using the same taker formula Kalshi publishes: 7% × contracts × price × (1 − price), rounded up to the next whole chip. It is biggest near 50¢. You can sell any time before settlement at the current bid. Charts show the last 2, 5 or 10 minutes.

    Every round lasts 5 minutes, then settles and a new round opens. The four event markets ask the same question every round, so each has one continuous chart: it jumps to 100 (YES) or 0 (NO) when a round settles, and the next round reopens at a fresh price. Gold vertical lines mark where each round starts. The BTC and Gold markets follow a simulated price that moves like the real thing (this is not live data). Each round asks whether the price will finish above the strike, and the dashed gold line on the chart is that strike. The YES price rises when the live price is above the strike and falls when it is below, and it snaps toward 99 or 1 as time runs out. When the round ends it settles against the final price, and the next round sets a fresh strike near the current price.

    Sun Guard · tower defense

    How to play (tap to hide)

    Smog blobs roll along the gold road. Stop them before they reach the right edge. Each one that gets through costs lives equal to its size, and you start every round with 40.

    1. Buy a tower with chips, tap the map to place it beside the road, then tap it to upgrade (3 levels).
    2. Buy ammo. Every shot uses ammo (shown on each tower card) and ammo costs chips. No ammo, no shooting.
    3. Send waves. Pop a blob and it shrinks a size, so big ones take several hits. Wave 10 brings an airship that splits into blobs.
    4. Win all 10 waves to finish the round and collect the round prize (shown next to your lives). Every 5th round is a solar-prize round: you also get back what you paid for every tower on your map. You keep your towers and leftover ammo for the harder next round. Lose and every tower and every bit of ammo is gone, with no prize. A round in progress is saved, so closing or reloading the page just pauses it.
    5. Need more chips? Press Pause during a wave (the game freezes, and it also waits between waves). Start the timer at the top and study, then buy towers, upgrades and ammo with what you earn and press Resume. Switching to another tab also pauses it.

    Chess · every move costs chips

    How this works (tap to hide)

    You play White against the house. Every game has a buy-in, and each move you make costs chips too (at least 50). The easier opponents are the expensive ones to play, and the hardest has the cheapest entry and the biggest prize. The win prize stays fixed until what you have spent on moves passes half of it. After that, every extra move you pay for is added to the prize, so a long game is always paid back if you win. Draw and you get half of everything you paid back, lose and it is all gone. Tap a piece, then tap where it should go. Castling, en passant and promotion (to a queen) all work.

    Play money only. Chips come from time at your desk and finished tasks, and nothing here touches real cash. Progress saves automatically.
